What's reported at Bristol

Gathered from public listings and player reports. House rules change, so read the placard on the felt before you buy in.

Blackjack

  • Six-deck shoes. Double-deck tables are also advertised.
  • Lower-limit tables ($10 and $15) pay 6 to 5 on blackjack. $25 tables and up pay 3 to 2.
  • Split up to four hands. Aces split once and get one card each.
  • The trainer assumes the dealer hits soft 17, doubles on any two cards and after splits, and offers no surrender. Bristol hasn't published these, so check the felt.

Roulette

  • Double-zero and triple-zero tables. Reports say the triple-zero table is the one usually open, and the double-zero tables open on busy nights.
  • Double zero has a 5.26% house edge. Triple zero has 7.69%. If both are open, play double zero.

Baccarat

  • Older listings include baccarat, but the casino's current table games page doesn't. Call ahead if it's the main game you want to play.
  • Most US casinos deal EZ Baccarat (no commission, with the Dragon 7 and Panda 8 side bets) or mini baccarat with a 5% commission. Both are here.

At the table

Buying in

  • Set cash flat on the felt between hands. Dealers can't take money from your hand. Say "change only" if you aren't betting yet.
  • You must be 21 or older. Have your ID and your players' card ready so your play counts toward comps.
  • Roulette gives you colored wheel chips that only work at that table. Cash them out with the dealer before you leave.

Blackjack signals

  • Hit: tap the felt behind your cards.
  • Stand: wave a flat hand over your cards.
  • Double or split: set a matching stack next to your bet, not on top of it. Point one finger to double. Make a V to split.
  • Don't touch cards dealt face up from a shoe, and don't touch your bet once the cards are out.

Roulette

  • Bets close when the dealer waves a hand over the layout and says "no more bets."
  • Wait until the dealer lifts the marker (the dolly) off the winning number before you reach for chips or bet again.

Baccarat

  • You just pick Player, Banker or Tie. The dealer handles every draw by fixed rules, so there are no decisions to get wrong.
  • Banker has the lowest house edge (1.06% with commission). Tie (14.4%) and the side bets are costly.
